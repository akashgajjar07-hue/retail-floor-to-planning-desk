Autumn/Winter season: transfer, markdown or hold

Seasonal lines are never reordered. Projected leftover = stock on hand − forecast demand to season end (31 Jan). Receive transfer: short by more than 20% of remaining demand. Send: long by more than 25% while another store is short.

Method, and what this does not prove

The data is synthetic, and I planted the problems in it myself. So the real question is not "what did I discover?" but "does the method find the planted problems, and rank them correctly?"

Planted problems and whether the method found them

Logic by product type

  • Basics (T-shirts, jeans, socks): reorder point = d × L + SS, with SS = z·√(L·σd² + d²·σL²) so supplier lead-time variability is inside the formula. Planned at style level, split to sizes by each store's own size curve.
  • Seasonal (Ramadan & Eid, Summer, Autumn/Winter): bought once. In season, a weekly review compares projected demand to stock and chooses transfer, markdown (20%, then 35%) or hold, with a 40% clearance in the last 10 days.
  • Accessories: core lines (caps, belts, scarves) are replenished like basics; seasonal ones (straw hats, beanies, Eid gift sets) are treated as seasonal.
  • No backorders. In a shop, a customer who cannot find the size leaves. Inventory position = on hand + on order + in transit.

Limitations

  • Synthetic data. Every result is a simulated scenario outcome, not a saving at any real retailer.
  • The planning calendar (Ramadan, Eid, school, promotions) is given to the model exactly; in practice those effects must be estimated.
  • One echelon: lead times run supplier → store via a DC that has a daily dispatch limit but never runs out of stock. Real networks have DC stock limits and allocation rules.
  • Lost sales are valued at lost margin only, with no customer-goodwill cost.
  • Seasonal markdown thresholds were set once by judgement and are not optimised; the Ramadan capsule shows no gain over a simple late markdown.
  • Results come from one seed plus five more as a robustness check, not a statistical proof.
